The one load-bearing finding: **there is no queryable API for route-level T-100
Segment data** (origin, destination, distance, carrier class, all in one row).
Every dataset BTS exposes through its Socrata portal at `data.bts.gov` is a
pre-aggregated summary (by origin airport, by carrier, by country, or
national) — none of them carries a destination airport. This directly affects
how literally we can answer the long-haul question (see below).

## A. T-100 Segment Summary by Origin Airport (`r495-tyji`)

- **Reachable:** yes, via Socrata SoQL. `data.transportation.gov` 302-redirects
  to `data.bts.gov` — use the latter directly.
  ```
  curl -s "https://data.bts.gov/resource/r495-tyji.json?\$limit=1"
  ```
- **Auth / rate limits:** none required for the volumes we need; an
  `X-App-Token` is optional and only matters past ~1000 req/rolling-window.
- **Granularity:** airport-month (one row per origin airport per
  `reporting_month`).
- **Fields (28):** `origin_airport_code`, `origin_airport_name`,
  `origin_city_name`, `reporting_month`, `year`, plus `total_*` and
  `domestic_*` variants of: `departures`, `passengers`, `seats`,
  `load_factor`, `passengers_flight`, `seats_flight`, `distance_flight_sm`
  (**average** stage length, not a distribution), `distance_passenger`,
  `payload_lbs`, `freight_lbs`, `mail_lbs`.
- **Date coverage:** 2014-01 through 2026-04 (131,739 rows total). ~2-month
  lag behind "today."
- **Coverage check — all 11 named airports present**, latest month 2026-04:
  SFO, LAX, SNA, ANC, JFK, BOS, BDL, PWM, PVD, BTV, MHT all returned rows.
- **Quirks:** values arrive as strings, not numbers (cast on ingest). No
  per-route breakdown — can't tell you *where* ANC's flights go, only
  aggregate totals in/out of that airport.
- **What it gives us:** growth, load factor and its trend, seat-vs-passenger
  growth gap, domestic/international split — all airport-month ratios, no
  double-counting risk since each field is a single well-defined ratio.

## B. T-100 Segment (All Carriers), route level — the long-haul source

This is the one genuine risk flagged in the plan, and it played out as
expected.

- **What we needed:** one row per (origin, destination, carrier/aircraft
  class, distance, departures, passengers) so long-haul % can be computed as
  "departures on routes ≥ threshold miles ÷ total departures," with
  freighters separable from passenger service.
- **What's on `data.bts.gov`:** searched the Socrata catalog scoped to that
  domain for every plausible name (`T-100`, `segment`, `market`, `nonstop`,
  `origin and destination`, `international segment`). Found five "AFF -
  T100 Segment Summary" datasets — **by origin airport, by carrier, by
  country, monthly-national, and plain-national** — and inspected every
  field list. **None has a destination-airport column.** They are BTS's own
  pre-aggregated rollups of the underlying microdata, not the microdata
  itself.
- **Where the real table lives:** `transtats.bts.gov`, behind
  `DL_SelectFields.aspx` (table ID in the 290s range). This is a classic
  ASP.NET WebForms page — `Fields.asp` and `DatabaseInfo.asp` both returned
  `HTTP 302` to a `nosessionvar.asp` redirect when hit without a live session
  cookie and viewstate token. Downloading programmatically means scripting a
  multi-step form POST (fetch page → parse hidden `__VIEWSTATE` →
  POST selected fields/month range → follow redirect to a generated
  `/PREZIP/<random-id>_T_T100_SEGMENT_ALL_CARRIER.zip`). We confirmed this
  pattern by finding exactly such files already sitting in `/PREZIP/` from
  other users' one-off 2015 sessions (e.g.
  `896816367_T_T100_SEGMENT_ALL_CARRIER.zip`, 3.5MB) — but the filename
  prefix is a session-specific random number, **not predictable by month**,
  so there's no stable URL to re-fetch a given month later the way OTP has.
- **Verdict: NO-GO as a clean, stable, auth-free API.** Available only via
  fragile, undocumented session/form automation — exactly the kind of
  brittleness flagged as a DON'T going into this. We will not build the
  agent's core long-haul tool on top of a scraped ASP.NET form.
- **Consequence for the long-haul question:** we cannot compute the literal,
  all-carrier, route-level long-haul % for ANC from a stable source. Section
  "ANC long-haul: what's actually computable" below spells out the
  decomposition we'll surface instead, labeled as a proxy with its specific
  blind spot, rather than presenting a wrong number as if it were the literal
  answer.

## C. BTS On-Time Performance (flight-level)

- **Reachable:** yes, as a predictable monthly bulk ZIP, no auth:
  ```
  https://transtats.bts.gov/PREZIP/On_Time_Reporting_Carrier_On_Time_Performance_1987_present_<YYYY>_<M>.zip
  ```
  Verified via `HEAD` for 2026-7: `HTTP 200`, `Content-Length: 33,075,361`
  bytes. Confirmed via directory listing that this exact predictable
  filename pattern exists for every month from 1987-10 through 2026-7 (latest
  at spike time — BTS trails "today," 2026-10-08, by about 3 months).
- **Granularity:** one row per scheduled flight. 631,970 rows for July 2026
  alone.
- **Fields (109 total; the ones we need):** `FlightDate`, `Reporting_Airline`,
  `Origin`, `Dest`, `DepDelay`/`DepDelayMinutes`/`DepDel15`, `TaxiOut`,
  `TaxiIn`, `Cancelled`, `CancellationCode`, `Distance`, `DistanceGroup`,
  `AirTime`, plus delay-cause breakdowns (`CarrierDelay`, `WeatherDelay`,
  `NASDelay`, `SecurityDelay`, `LateAircraftDelay`).
- **Scope limitation (critical caveat, confirmed empirically):** OTP only
  covers **DOT-reporting passenger carriers operating domestic segments**.
  Pulling every `Origin == 'ANC'` row for July 2026 returned only 2,513
  flights from five carriers: `AA, AS, DL, UA, WN` — zero freighter
  carriers. ANC is one of the world's busiest cargo hubs (FedEx, UPS, Polar,
  Atlas, Cargolux, etc., mostly to/from Asia); none of that traffic appears
  in OTP. This confirms, with real numbers rather than assumption, that OTP
  cannot stand in for the literal long-haul question at ANC, and that any
  congestion metric for an international/cargo-heavy airport should note the
  same blind spot.
- **Payload size:** ~33MB zipped, ~286MB unzipped CSV per month. One month
  was downloaded to `/tmp` to inspect real fields and numbers (not committed
  to the repo, per decision #4).
- **Verdict: GO** for congestion (delay, taxi-out, cancellation rate), with
  an explicit "domestic reporting-carrier only" caveat baked into every
  congestion tool response.
- **Real congestion numbers, July 2026** (computed directly from the
  downloaded file, not invented):

  | Airport | Flights | Cancelled | Mean taxi-out | Mean dep delay | % dep delay > 15min |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | SFO | 13,841 | 159 | 25.1 min | 24.7 min | 34.3% |
  | LAX | 17,454 | 210 | 18.8 min | 18.7 min | 24.8% |
  | SNA | 3,992 | 45 | 16.8 min | 20.2 min | 25.0% |

  Useful finding: SNA's raw delay/taxi profile is **not** obviously calmer
  than LAX's despite being a fraction of the size — exactly why the design
  needs a separate "buildability" flag (SNA's settlement-agreement caps)
  rather than relying on congestion metrics alone to explain why SNA isn't a
  good expansion candidate.

## D. OurAirports reference list

- **Reachable:** yes, static CSV, no auth, updated daily
  (`last-modified` header showed same-day).
  ```
  https://davidmegginson.github.io/ourairports-data/airports.csv
  ```
- **Size:** 12.7MB, 86,224 rows (worldwide, all airport types).
- **Fields:** `ident` (ICAO), `iata_code`, `icao_code`, `name`,
  `iso_country`, `iso_region` (state, e.g. `US-MA`), `municipality`,
  `latitude_deg`/`longitude_deg`, `scheduled_service` (yes/no).
- **Quirks found:**
  - BTS datasets key on **IATA** (`SFO`), OurAirports' primary `ident` column
    is **ICAO** (`KSFO`) — must join on the separate `iata_code` field, which
    is empty for 20 of 699 US scheduled-service airports (~3%).
  - `scheduled_service = yes` **over-includes**: pulling every CT/ME/MA/NH/RI/VT
    airport with that flag returns 24 airports, including fields like
    Block Island (BID), Westerly (WST), Rutland (RUT) that have no real
    airline (T-100) traffic. Region membership must be the **intersection**
    of this list with airports actually present in source A, not this flag
    alone — confirms decision to resolve regions in code against real
    traffic data, not a reference-list flag.
- **Verdict: GO**, with that intersection rule noted for Phase 1.

## ANC long-haul: what's actually computable

Literal answer (all-carrier, route-level, freighters separable): **not
computable** from a stable API (source B is NO-GO; see above).

What we *can* surface, decomposed and labeled, not substituted for the
literal answer:

- **MEASURED (domestic passenger-carrier only, source C):** of 2,513
  domestic passenger flights out of ANC in July 2026, 40.8% flew ≥2,000mi,
  24.8% ≥2,500mi, 8.8% ≥3,000mi. Explicitly labeled as covering only
  AA/AS/DL/UA/WN — the five domestic passenger carriers DOT requires to
  report — and explicitly **excluding** all international passenger routes
  and all freighter traffic.
- **INFERRED (source A, international/domestic split):** ANC's
  `outbound_international`/`inbound_international` fields in source A show
  there is substantial international departure volume beyond the 2,513 OTP
  rows (to be quantified in Phase 1); since Anchorage's international
  departures are almost entirely transpacific (long-haul by distance), this
  pushes the true long-haul share meaningfully above the domestic-only
  40.8%, but we cannot state a precise corrected percentage without
  route-level distances.
- **UNKNOWN:** the freighter share of ANC's departures and their distance
  distribution — structurally absent from every source checked.

This decomposition — not a single invented percentage — is what the
long-haul tool will return for ANC, each line tagged MEASURED / INFERRED /
UNKNOWN per the design principles.

## Empirical minimum-volume floor

Computed from real Jan-2024 → Jan-2025 year-over-year domestic-passenger
growth across 710 airports with data in both months (source A):

| Monthly domestic pax (base) | n | mean growth | stdev | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1,000 | 352 | +64% | 3.49 | −99% | +5,500% |
| 1,000–10,000 | 152 | +9% | 0.32 | −64% | +181% |
| 10,000–50,000 | 92 | +9% | 0.15 | −60% | +57% |
| 50,000–200,000 | 57 | +4% | 0.07 | −11% | +24% |
| 200,000–1,000,000 | 38 | −2% | 0.07 | −23% | +17% |
| 1,000,000+ | 19 | +1% | 0.05 | −5% | +11% |

Variance falls off a cliff below 10,000 monthly domestic passengers (a single
small airport swung +5,500% YoY) and keeps dropping meaningfully through
50,000. **Recommended floor: 10,000–20,000 monthly domestic passengers**,
which keeps 182–210 airports (of 843 total reporting that month) — enough
for meaningful peer groups within hub tiers, small enough to exclude the
noise. This was a single seasonal month (Jan); Phase 1 will confirm the
floor against a trailing-12-month average before it's locked in, per the
"fixed before we look at rankings" rule.

## Hub-tier share sanity check

National domestic passengers, Jan-2025 (single month, for scale only):
60,832,939. At the proposed thresholds that implies Large ≥ 608,329/month,
Medium ≥ 152,082/month, Small ≥ 30,416/month. These are directionally
sane (SFO/LAX/BOS land comfortably in Large; BDL/PVD/PWM/MHT/BTV land in
progressively smaller tiers) but must be computed from a **trailing 12-month
sum**, not one seasonal month, before Phase 1 finalizes tier cutoffs —
FAA's own CY enplanement methodology is annual, and a January share isn't
comparable to it.
